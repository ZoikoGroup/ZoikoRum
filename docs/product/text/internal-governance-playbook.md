## 0. PURPOSE

This Playbook defines how Zoikorum enforces governance in reality, not theory.

It exists to ensure:

- Consistent decisions across teams

- No silent or arbitrary enforcement

- UX-safe execution of policy

- Audit-defensible actions

- Human accountability at every step

This document is binding for all Zoikorum staff and contractors.

## 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE UI / UX LAWS (INTERNAL)

Governance enforcement must feel fair, not punitive.

| UX / UI Law | Internal Enforcement Application |
|---|---|
| Jakob’s Law | Enforcement flows resemble standard enterprise patterns |
| Hick’s Law | ≤3 actions presented to users per decision |
| Miller’s Law | ≤7 visible facts during enforcement |
| Fitts’s Law | Help, appeal, and explanation always accessible |
| Progressive Disclosure | Severity revealed step-by-step |
| Error Prevention | Block harm before enforcement |
| Recognition > Recall | Visual states, timelines, reason codes |
| Transparency Heuristic | Always answer “Why?” |
| Peak-End Rule | Clear resolution confirmation |
| WCAG 2.2 AAA (Target) | Enforcement accessible to all |

## 3. ENFORCEMENT PRINCIPLES (MANDATORY)

All actions must be:

- Explainable

- Proportionate

- Reversible where possible

- Logged immutably

- Consistent across cases

If an action cannot be explained clearly to a user, it must not be taken.

## 4. INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION MODEL

### Level 0 — Informational

- Clarification required

- No enforcement

- Educational messaging only

### Level 1 — Minor Violation

- Warning

- No access restriction

- Logged for trend analysis

### Level 2 — Moderate Risk

- Temporary restrictions

- Remediation required

- Buyer visibility adjustments

### Level 3 — High Risk

- Engagement suspension

- Credential enforcement

- Buyer notification required

### Level 4 — Severe / Legal

- Account offboarding

- Law enforcement or regulator referral

- Executive + Legal approval required

## 5. STANDARD ENFORCEMENT FLOW (MANDATORY)

Signal Detected

## 6. SIGNAL SOURCES

Signals may originate from:

- Automated detection

- Credential expiry

- Buyer reports

- Professional reports

- Enterprise escalations

- Regulatory notifications

- Manual audits

All signals must be triaged within SLA.

## 7. UX-SAFE USER NOTIFICATION STANDARD

Every enforcement notice must include:

- What happened

- Why it happened

- What changed

- What the user can do

- What happens next

- How to get help

No legal jargon. No blame language. No ambiguity.

## 8. PROFESSIONAL-SIDE ENFORCEMENT

### Allowed Controls

- Visibility reduction

- Engagement suspension

- Credential enforcement

- Verification reset

- Offboarding

### Prohibited Actions

- Silent ranking penalties

- Unexplained removals

- Retroactive punishment without notice

## 9. BUYER-SIDE ENFORCEMENT

Buyers may face:

- Warning notices

- Engagement limits

- Dispute access restrictions

- Account suspension

Buyers must always retain:

- Access to explanations

- Ability to resolve issues

- Due process

## 10. DISPUTE HANDLING GOVERNANCE

### Required Steps

- Structured filing

- Evidence validation

- Neutral review

- Timed response windows

- Outcome explanation

- Record finalization

No dispute is closed without documented reasoning.

## 11. APPEALS & OVERRIDES

### Appeal Eligibility

- Enforcement ≥ Level 2

- Credential disputes

- Engagement suspensions

- Offboarding actions

### Appeal Rules

- Independent reviewer required

- Evidence-based only

- Time-bounded

- One final decision

## 12. HUMAN-IN-COMMAND REQUIREMENT

AI may:

- Flag risks

- Suggest actions

- Summarize evidence

AI may not:

- Decide enforcement

- Resolve disputes

- Approve credentials

- Override humans

Every decision has a named human owner.

## 13. AUDIT & LOGGING STANDARD

Each action must log:

- Timestamp

- Actor (human/system)

- Reason code

- Evidence references

- Policy version applied

- User notification content

Logs are immutable and retained per policy.

## 14. METRICS & INTERNAL KPIs

Tracked weekly:

- Enforcement volume by level

- Reversal rate

- Appeal success rate

- Time-to-resolution

- User satisfaction post-resolution

- Recurring violation patterns

Metrics are used to improve fairness, not increase punishment.
