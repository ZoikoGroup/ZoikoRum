# Executive Positioning

## What Zoikorum Is

Zoikorum is professional engagement infrastructure that enables enterprises to engage external professionals under verifiable eligibility, enforceable contracts, protected payments, and audit-grade records — before work begins, not after risk emerges.

## What Zoikorum Is Not

- A freelance marketplace

- A job board

- A gig economy platform

- A speed-optimized talent discovery tool

## Institutional Category Role

Zoikorum operates as a system of record for professional services engagements — infrastructure enterprises rely on when accountability, governance, and auditability are non-negotiable.

Positioning note: Category-role analogy only. No implied equivalence, endorsement, or certification.

## Core Value Proposition (Locked)

Professional services engagements — verified, contracted, and governed by default.

Zoikorum reduces preventable legal, financial, and operational risk by making verification, contracts, payment protection, and evidence generation prerequisites — not optional features.

## Non-Negotiable Operating Principles

- Governance precedes convenience.

- Risk is addressed before engagement.

- Verification, contracts, and payments are inseparable.

- Every engagement produces a defensible record.

- AI assists; policy constrains; humans decide.

- No engagement without accountability on both sides.

Homepage rule: If a claim cannot be defended simultaneously to a General Counsel, CFO, and procurement committee, it must not appear as a core promise.

# 3. Trust Tiers and Verification System

## 3.2 Trust Tier Framework

| Tier | Designation | Eligibility | Key Restrictions / Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier A | Fully Verified Professional | Enterprise and high-control engagements (per policy) | Eligibility evidence available; may require credentials, jurisdiction, restrictions screening, insurance (policy-dependent). |

# 4. Dual-Sided Governance (Primary Differentiator)

## 4.1 Headline and Purpose

Zoikorum governs both sides of the engagement. Authority is explicit, approvals are structured, and exceptions are controlled — so accountability is never assumed.

## 4.2 Enterprise Controls

- Role authority: requester vs approver vs budget owner.

- Acceptance authority: who may accept milestones and close work.

- Policy exception authority: who can override controls and under what conditions.

- Dispute authority: who may file, respond, approve resolution, and release funds.

- Delegation rules with time bounds and audit logging.

- Immutable authorization log included in the engagement dossier.

## 4.3 Professional Controls

- Eligibility enforcement (tier + dimensions required by policy).

- Contract compliance requirements and required attestations.

- Submission, versioning, and acceptance history.

- Dispute participation rules and evidence submission standards.

- Payout eligibility tied to acceptance gates and policy conditions.

- Professional integrity record based on outcomes, not social ratings.

# 9. How It Works — Governed Lifecycle

- Define engagement need (scope, outcomes, success criteria).

- Apply enterprise policy profile (eligibility, contracts, controls).

- Source eligible professionals (only policy-compliant options displayed).

- Execute contract and fund escrow (per policy).

- Deliver, review, close with an audit-grade engagement record.

## 9.1 Dispute Handling (Branch Path)

- Either party files dispute with evidence.

- Funds held per escrow terms and policy.

- Resolution pathway: review → mediation → arbitration (if applicable).

- Outcome documented; disposition recorded; dossier updated.
